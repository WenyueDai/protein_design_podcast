That’s promising, but the leave‑one‑recording‑out cross‑validation they use still risks over‑optimistic estimates if there’s hidden heterogeneity – say, differences in maternal BMI or gestational age at recording. I’d love to see them repeat the experiment on a completely independent cohort, maybe from a different hospital, and report not just accuracy but calibration curves. Are the predicted probabilities well‑calibrated, or does the model systematically over‑confident for the preterm class? Tracking a reliability diagram alongside the AUC would tell us whether we could trust those probabilities for clinical risk thresholds.

All of these threads point to a few concrete next steps: pull the raw DAAO mutant fitness scores if they’re available and recompute epistatic interaction metrics; run FTMap on a panel of viral antigens and compare hot spots to a database of known epitopes; augment the sewing thread dataset with a few cotton or polyester swatches and see if the Extra Trees model needs retraining; synthesize the C2 and C6 AFM1 haptens and measure their binding to a panel of anti‑AFM1 antibodies; and obtain a larger EHG set, perhaps from the open‑access TERM‑PREG repository, to test whether IMF1 still outperforms time‑domain features when you control for recording length and electrode placement. Each of these steps would turn an interesting observation into a tighter, more generalizable design principle – exactly the kind of iterative loop that keeps protein engineering and biomedical ML moving forward.
