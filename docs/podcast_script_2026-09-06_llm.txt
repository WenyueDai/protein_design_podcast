Section 7 – Tensions, contradictions, and healthy skepticism  

The NarL‑ogt promoter study reports a binding free energy of −9.42 kcal mol⁻¹ measured by ITC and EMSA, yet it never shows that this affinity translates into physiological occupancy under the anaerobic concentrations NarL actually reaches inside Salmonella Typhimurium; a ΔG of −9.4 kcal mol⁻¹ corresponds to a Kd in the low‑micromolar range, but the paper does not quantify NarL cellular levels or test whether the ogt promoter is occupied in vivo by chromatin immunoprecipitation, leaving open the possibility that the observed binding is an artefact of over‑expressed protein in vitro.  

In contrast, the MAPKAPK2 inhibitor framework claims a ten‑model voting ensemble reached 0.969 accuracy on a held‑out test set, but the notes admit that the size and chemical diversity of the in‑house compound library are undisclosed; if the library is small and biased toward scaffolds already known to bind kinases, the high accuracy could simply reflect memorisation of chemotypes rather than genuine predictive power, and the lack of external validation on an independent dataset or on structurally novel scaffolds makes it impossible to rule out over‑fitting.  

The paper on PrkA/YeaG from Thermus thermophilus presents cryo‑EM structures and biochemical assays that suggest chaperone‑like activity, yet it simultaneously notes that the protein’s kinase activity remains elusive; this creates an internal tension because the same structural fold that enables ATP binding in typical kinases is invoked to argue for a chaperone role without demonstrating either loss of kinase function or gain of chaperone function in a direct side‑by‑side assay, so the claim rests on the assumption that structural similarity alone predicts functional repurposing.  

Both the pediatric fracture AI study and the melanoma drug‑repurposing GraphPert paper provide virtually no methodological specifics — no dataset sizes, no performance metrics, no description of the graph autoencoder architecture — making it impossible to assess whether their reported successes stem from favourable benchmark splits, leakage, or simply from evaluating on trivial cases; without concrete numbers such as cross‑validation AUC, test‑set prevalence, or ablation studies, any claim of generalisability remains speculative.  

To move beyond skepticism, each study would need to complement its core assay with an orthogonal validation: NarL should be tested for promoter occupancy under native anaerobic conditions using ChIP‑qPCR; the MAPKAPK2 ensemble should be screened against an external, structurally diverse library and the hit S021‑0180 should undergo solubility and permeability assays; PrkA/YeaG requires a direct kinase activity read‑out alongside a chaperone refolding assay; and the AI papers must disclose dataset characteristics, report confidence intervals, and show performance on temporally or geographically held‑out data. Only then could we reconcile the apparent contradictions and decide whether the observed effects are robust or artefacts of favourable assumptions.
