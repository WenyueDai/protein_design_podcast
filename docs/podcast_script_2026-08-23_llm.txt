The PINN study on HVOF‑sprayed carbon‑based coatings gave us a tangible number: the residual stress is compressive around ‑270 MPa at the coating‑substrate interface and tensile near the free surface, creating a steep gradient through the thickness. That raises the question of how sensitive that ‑270 MPa value is to the process parameters they highlighted — particle velocity, temperature, spray angle, substrate pre‑heating. A systematic sensitivity analysis, varying each parameter by ±10 % while holding the others fixed, could map out a response surface that tells us, for example, whether a 5 % increase in particle velocity pushes the interfacial stress toward ‑300 MPa or whether substrate pre‑heating has a negligible effect.  

Because PINNs embed the governing PDEs directly, they promise lower computational cost than full FEM sweeps, yet the paper notes they still need some data and can be vulnerable to generalization gaps. What failure modes should we watch for when we push a PINN beyond the temperature range it was trained on? Does the network start to violate the embedded elasticity equations, producing non‑physical negative moduli, or does it simply drift toward the prior imposed by the loss weighting? Running a PINN trained on‑the‑fly with synthetic FEM data at 800 K, then testing it at 1200 K, and monitoring the residual of the heat‑equation term would make those failure modes concrete.  

Finally, the Avelumab/PD‑L1 HDX‑NMR work reminds us that solution‑state dynamics can hide epitopes that crystal structures miss. If we wanted to extend that approach to a bispecific antibody, what combination of deuterium exchange time points and NMR chemical‑shift perturbations would give us the clearest picture of simultaneous binding to both targets? Designing a pulse‑label experiment that captures fast (10 s), medium (10 min) and slow (10 h) exchange regimes, then mapping the protection patterns onto a homology model of each Fab, could reveal whether binding at one site allosterically shields or exposes the other — a question that structural models alone struggle to answer.  

These threads — ensemble sensitivity, architecture selection, process‑parameter mapping, extrapolation robustness, and dynamic epitope mapping — feel like the next set of concrete experiments worth running.
