The paper reports median seconds per ligand but doesn’t break down the cost of generating the conformer set itself — tracking that preprocessing time could reveal whether the real bottleneck is the conformer search or the docking scoring, guiding us to invest in faster conformer generators or smarter clustering of starting poses.

For RNA secondary structure, the call for expanded training data makes me curious about the impact of incorporating experimental probing data — like SHAPE‑Seq reactivity profiles — into the loss function. If we took a benchmark set such as RNAStrAlgo’s 200‑structure test and added pseudo‑energy terms derived from 10 000 in‑vivo SHAPE experiments, would the F1 score on base‑pair prediction jump from the reported 0.68 to over 0.80? And what failure modes emerge when the training data over‑represents certain motifs — like hairpins — causing the model to miss pseudoknots in viral genomes?

The cryptic binding site work with equivariant GNNs and Kolmogorov‑Arnold networks raises a methodological puzzle: how sensitive is the CBS prediction to the choice of the equivariant layer’s degree? If we replace the standard SE(3)‑Transformer with a simpler tensor‑field network that only respects rotations, does the AUC drop by 0.05, or does the added flexibility of the Kolmogorov‑Arnold blocks compensate for lost translation equivariance? Running an ablation where we train on the Apo‑holo pairs from the BindingDB CBS subset and test on a held‑out set of 150 kinase structures could reveal whether the model truly learns conformational ensembles or merely memorizes holo‑state pockets.

Finally, the immunochromatographic assay for PSA and α‑amylase is intriguing but the full text isn’t accessible; reaching out to the authors for a preprint or requesting the antibody pair sequences would let us evaluate the assay’s limit of detection — reportedly in the low‑ng/mL range for each analyte — and see if cross‑talk between the two test lines becomes a problem at high concentrations of one biomarker. That kind of quantitative specificity data would be essential if we wanted to adapt the strip format to other forensic mixtures, like saliva‑semen blends in sexual assault kits.
