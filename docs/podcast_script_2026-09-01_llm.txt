Together, these works shift the mindset from accepting opaque scores and massive data files to demanding transparent, queryable outputs and predictors that finally recognize the rare but valuable gains engineers seek.

The probabilistic interaction score introduced in the first paper feels like a modern revival of the Bayesian scoring functions that early docking programs used to weigh geometric fit against solvation terms, except here the prior comes directly from the distribution of inter‑residue distances in AlphaFold’s multiple sequence alignments. That echoes the way the original AlphaFold2 paper treated contact maps as noisy observations of a underlying potential of mean force, turning a structural prediction into a statistical inference problem. By converting the aligned distance distribution into a simple likelihood, the authors are effectively estimating a posterior probability of interaction without re‑training a deep net, which mirrors how physicists extract binding free energies from umbrella sampling histograms using the weighted histogram analysis method.

FoldKit’s compression of AlphaFold 3 co‑folding outputs by 5‑15‑fold is not just a software engineering win; it is an illustration of information theory in action.
